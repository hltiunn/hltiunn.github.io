I study how critical national systems hold up under pressure: the health workforce that keeps American care running, and the defense supply chains that Taiwan and its partners depend on.

Policy researcher who applies a single discipline — rigorous quantitative policy evaluation paired with legislative translation and international coordination — to the resilience of critical national systems. Trained as a health policy researcher (PhD candidate, George Washington University; dissertation defended November 2026), I built that methodology studying the health workforce, then extended it to the defense industrial base as Deputy Director of the National Security Program at Taiwan's DSET. The same analytic toolkit that produced peer-reviewed workforce research (JAMA Network Open, Health Affairs Scholar; 165 citations, h-index 6) also produced the flagship UAV supply-chain report Drones for Democracy, cited in Bloomberg, The Economist, and Deutsche Welle. IVLP Fellow (U.S. Department of State) and AcademyHealth Board member. My work advances U.S. national interest by strengthening the resilience of the systems the country depends on — its health workforce and its defense supply chains.

How does a critical system withstand shocks, and which policy levers build its resilience?

The health policy doctoral training supplies the formal methodological foundation; the national-security work is that foundation deployed on a second critical system, backed by a flagship report, sustained media authority, and a standing think-tank appointment.

Quantitative policy evaluation (difference-in-differences, event studies, econometrics) — used to measure how the nurse licensure compact shifts interstate mobility and to assess fragility in UAV component supply chains.
Legislative translation — eight years turning technical evidence into enacted law and policy options in Taiwan's parliament, applied equally to health, labor, environmental, and defense bills.
International coordination — liaison work with WTO/ADB and allied-partner engagement on U.S.–Taiwan defense cooperation.

Current research

2022–present

Research Specialist / Research Associate

Fitzhugh Mullan Institute for Health Workforce Equity, The George Washington University, Washington, DC

Research staff at the GW Health Workforce Research Center, an HRSA-funded Health Workforce Research Center (HWRC) housed within the Mullan Institute — a core part of my doctoral training, carried out in parallel with (and distinct from) my independent dissertation. The center is supported by the Bureau of Health Workforce (BHW), National Center for Health Workforce Analysis (NCHWA), Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA), U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. Analyzed national Medicaid and workforce data (T-MSIS, UDS, NPPES, NSSRN, ACS, AHA Annual Survey) in Stata/SAS/Python to inform HRSA primary-care and safety-net workforce policy.

  • School-Based Health Centers (SBHCs) and Medicaid-enrolled children (first author) — first national study of federally sponsored SBHCs using T-MSIS (2016–2020, 16 states, ages 5–18). Found FQHC-sponsored SBHCs nearly doubled (+98%) over the period; SBHC-exposed students in frontier areas had ~15% higher primary-care and well-child visit rates (rural ~5–6% higher), while urban areas showed no comparable gain; MOUD prescribing rose from <4% to ~14%. Presented at AcademyHealth (2025–2026) and ASHEcon (2026).
  • Interstate nurse mobility and the enhanced Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC) (first author) — quantified how compact membership shifts cross-state nursing employment; published in Health Affairs Scholar (qxag221).
  • Community health worker (CHW) Medicaid billing — first national study tracking CHW direct Medicaid billing; found a 638% increase in CHWs billing Medicaid (99 to 731) across nine states, 2016–2020, with service volume growing nearly 23-fold; published in Health Affairs Scholar (qxae164).
  • Overtime and agency-nurse reliance and patient safety (co-author) — evaluated the effect of contingent staffing on patient-safety outcomes; published in JAMA Network Open (2025).
  • Hospital financial resources and nurse staffing through COVID-19 (co-author) — published in Nursing Outlook (2025).
  • Community health center (CHC) vertical integration (contributor) — assessed effects on utilization and care quality; presented at ASHEcon (2026).
  • Translated findings into policy recommendations and delivered HRSA briefings.
